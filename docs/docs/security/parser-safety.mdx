# Parser safety

Every adapter in this repository is handed bytes somebody else produced. This page is the policy
those parsers are held to. It is written for the formats **Part 2** brings — AIXM and KML (XML),
KMZ (a zip), GeoPackage (a zip and a SQLite database) — because those are the ones where a
parser's default behaviour is dangerous, and it is enforced today on the nineteen adapters that
ship — since 2026-09-20/21 that roster includes the Part 2 formats it was written for: AIXM 5.1.1
and 5.2 and C2SIM (XML), GeoPackage (a SQLite database) and GeoJSON.

The convention is the specification's: **MUST**, **SHOULD**, **MAY**.

## 1. The five bounds, and their one home

`ARCHITECTURE.md` §3.5 puts the bounds inside `capabilities`, and the reason is that there must be
exactly one declaration of each: the conformance suite's resource-limits check and this policy have
to read the same number, and two declarations of one bound is the arrangement where a parser is
hardened against a figure the report does not print.

| bound | what it bounds |
|---|---|
| `max_input_bytes` | the octets an adapter is handed for one payload |
| `max_depth` | how deeply a document may nest |
| `max_objects` | how many CDM objects one payload may produce |
| `max_decompressed_bytes` | what an archive may expand to |
| `max_parse_seconds` | wall time for one decode |

> An adapter **MUST** declare every one of the five, either as a number or as absent with a
> reason. `Limits` refuses a bare `None`: `absent_because` carries the reason, keyed by field.

> An adapter that declares a bound **MUST** record its basis. `declared_because` carries, per
> field: `kind` (`normative` or `implementation_cap`), the `source` the figure comes from, the
> `enforced_at` point, and the `test` that proves the refusal.

> A bound **MUST NOT** be described as the format's normative maximum unless the specification
> actually says so. Fourteen of the nineteen adapters declare an implementation cap and say so in
> the enum and in the prose; the five ASTERIX categories declare the normative `LEN` field.

Which bound runs before which allocation — size before the depth scan, the depth scan before
`json.loads`, the XML tree measured only after expat has built it — and what the conformance
worker and the hosting application each add on top is the subject of
`docs/docs/security/deployment-envelope.mdx` (audit finding F06). The boundary cases — a payload
*at* each bound and one octet or one level past it, in every octet form — are
`tests/test_cdm_resource_envelope.py`.

### What is enforced today

`max_input_bytes` is declared and enforced on **all nineteen**. `max_depth` is declared and enforced
on the **five XML readers** — `tak` and `stanag4676` since 2026-09-16, and `c2sim`, `aixm511` and
`aixm52` since 2026-09-21, the last three inside expat's start-element handler through
`secure_xml` — on the **five that decode JSON** — `adsb`, `ais`, `legion` and `pntmap`
since 2026-09-17, `geojson` since 2026-09-20 — and on `geopackage` (the parsed twin it also
accepts; the SQLite octets themselves have no nesting to measure, 2026-09-20).
`max_objects` is declared and enforced on the five of the adapter expansion (`geojson`,
`geopackage`, `c2sim`, `aixm511`, `aixm52`), each before the objects it bounds are built, and
declared absent with a reason on the other fourteen. `max_decompressed_bytes` and
`max_parse_seconds` are declared absent with a reason on all nineteen, and `max_depth` on the other
eight; none of those is enforced, and no page here says otherwise.

Enforcement of `max_input_bytes` is in the base class, at class-definition time:
`Adapter.__init_subclass__` wraps each subclass's own `to_cdm`, so the bound is checked before any
adapter's decoder runs, and an adapter cannot forget it while remaining an `Adapter`. A payload
over the bound raises `InputTooLarge`, whose message names both numbers.

Enforcement of `max_depth` is in two places, and which one depends on where the danger is.

For **JSON text and for a parsed document** it is in the base class, beside the size bound: the
same wrapper calls `enforce_depth_bound`, which reads the nesting off the octets
(`json_nesting_depth`) or off the containers (`container_depth`) and raises `InputTooDeep` — a
`ValueError` naming both numbers — before any adapter's decoder runs. *Before* the decoder, because
on CPython 3.11 `json.loads` itself recurses once per container and raises `RecursionError` a
little under a thousand containers deep — 995 read from module level on 3.11.15 under the default
limit, fewer beneath the frames a test runner adds — and no interpreter in the matrix decodes an
arbitrary depth (3.12.13 raises it near ten thousand, 3.14.7 near a hundred and sixteen thousand;
the readings are of 2026-09-17, the day the 3.11 leg of the CI matrix first ran, and
`adapter.InputTooDeep` carries them). So a bound measured after the decode was a bound the decoder
could fail before. The text is read in one pass and decoded the way `json.loads` would decode it —
a byte-order mark or a UTF-16 document is measured, not walked past.

> An adapter decoding JSON **MUST** declare `max_depth`, and the text **MUST** be measured before
> the decoder sees it. Here the parser *is* what recurses — on CPython 3.11 — so a bound applied
> to the decoded document is applied too late.

For an **XML tree** it is in the two adapters themselves, immediately after `ET.fromstring` returns
and before anything walks the tree, and the reason it is there and not in the base class is that
only the adapter holds the tree. libexpat builds a tree of **any** depth without recursing — a
document nested fifty thousand elements deep parses — and everything that walks the tree afterwards
recurses once per level. So until 2026-09-16 a *valid* CoT event or NITS document nesting a thousand
empty elements — some 7 KB, against a 1 MiB `max_input_bytes` — made both adapters raise
`RecursionError`, which is one of the conformance suite's four crash classes and not a refusal.
Each XML adapter now measures the tree with a stack the moment it exists and refuses past **64**
with its own `ValueError` naming both numbers.

The figure is **64** on all six and it is an **implementation cap** — each `declared_because` says
so: every CoT fixture in the package nests three elements deep, the deepest path AEDP-12's class
model admits is seven classes (eight elements at the deepest scalar), no JSON document shipped under
the four fixture directories, goldens included, nests more than seven containers (the deepest input
`to_cdm` is handed nests five), and sixty-four keeps every walker under two hundred
Python frames from any call depth beneath it. Each XML adapter also ships a `malformed/` payload
nested one thousand deep, so check **H** records the refusal on every run.

> `max_input_bytes` is on **octets**. A `dict` — the `.parsed.json` twin that ships beside a byte
> fixture — is **not** bounded by it, and this is a rule rather than an oversight: a caller holding
> a parsed document has already done the parse the bound exists to prevent. ADS-B's 14 wire octets
> serialise to over 400 as a decoded twin, so a bound applied to twins would refuse the format's
> own legal messages. `max_depth` is different in kind: the walk it protects happens *after* any
> parse, so the base class holds the parsed form of every adapter that declares the bound to the
> same number — `stanag4676`'s included, although that reader never descends past what the class
> model names.

## 2. XML

Part 2's AIXM and KML are XML, and so are two adapters that shipped before it — `tak` and
`stanag4676`, both through the standard library's `xml.etree.ElementTree`. Since 2026-09-21 three
Part 2 adapters parse XML as well — `c2sim`, `aixm511` and `aixm52` — through one shared reader,
`secure_xml.parse` (`pyexpat` driven directly: a DOCTYPE, an entity declaration, an external
entity and XInclude are refused in the handlers, the input is bounded before a parser exists, and
depth and element count are enforced in `StartElementHandler`; `tests/test_cdm_secure_xml.py`).

> An XML parser **MUST NOT** resolve external entities.

> An XML parser **MUST NOT** fetch an external DTD or schema, or make any other network request
> while parsing.

> An XML document **MUST** be bounded by `max_input_bytes` before it reaches the parser.

> An adapter parsing XML **MUST** ship tests for a malformed document, an external entity
> reference and an entity-expansion bomb, and **MUST** record its parser choice as a limitation
> where that choice leaves a property to the runtime.

> An adapter parsing XML **MUST** declare `max_depth` and refuse a deeper document before anything
> walks the tree. The parser is not what recurses; the walk is, and a document inside
> `max_input_bytes` can be deep enough to exhaust the interpreter's stack.

### What the standard library actually does — measured, not recited

These are readings taken in round P5 on CPython 3.14.7 with **libexpat 2.7.4**, not claims from
the documentation:

| case | `xml.etree.ElementTree` |
|---|---|
| external entity (`<!ENTITY xxe SYSTEM "file:///etc/passwd">`) | **refused** — `ParseError: undefined entity`. The external subset is never read, so the entity was never declared. |
| external DTD (`<!DOCTYPE foo SYSTEM "http://…">`) | **not fetched** — the document parses, the URL is never opened |
| small internal entity (3 levels, ×10) | **expanded**, silently, to 3 000 characters |
| entity bomb (6 levels, ×10) | **refused** — `ParseError: limit on input amplification factor (from DTD and entities) breached` |
| fifty thousand nested elements (read 2026-09-16) | **parsed** — the tree is built without recursing; what raised `RecursionError` was the adapter's own walk over it, which is what `max_depth` now bounds |

Two conclusions follow, and both matter more than the reassuring half.

The first is that the XXE case is stronger than "does not resolve": the reference is an **error**,
not an empty string, so a document that depends on one cannot pass silently.

The second is that **what stops an entity bomb is libexpat and not this package**. That protection
was added in libexpat 2.4.0 and is on by default; a deployment linking an older expat loses it
without anything in this package changing. That is why it is recorded as a known limitation on
both XML adapters rather than presented as a property of the package, and why
`tests/test_cdm_parser_safety.py` **reads** `pyexpat.version_info` and fails if it is older than
2.4.0, rather than assuming the build it runs on.

### `defusedxml`

`defusedxml` is **not** a dependency. It is deferred to Part 2, under a ruling that made the
deferral conditional on the four properties above being established by test rather than asserted —
which is what the table is. What it would add is the removal of the runtime dependency: it refuses
DTDs and entity declarations outright, in this package's own code, instead of relying on the expat
the deployment happens to link.

The declared version floor is Python 3.11. The readings above are from 3.14.7; the expat a 3.11
build links is a property of that build and not of the Python version, which is the whole reason
the test reads it at runtime.

## 3. Archives — Part 2 (KMZ, GeoPackage)

No shipped adapter opens an archive today: a sweep of `packages/cdm/synapse_cdm/adapters/` for
`zipfile`, `gzip` and `zlib` returns nothing. The rules are written now so that the first adapter
that needs one is held to them rather than setting the precedent.

> An archive-reading adapter **MUST** refuse any member whose name is absolute, or contains `..`,
> or resolves outside the extraction root — checked on the resolved path, not on the name as
> stored.

> It **MUST** declare `max_decompressed_bytes` and enforce it on the **running total** of what has
> been written, not on the header's declared size. A header is attacker-controlled.

> It **MUST** enforce a decompression **ratio** bound as well as a total, and **MUST** decompress
> in bounded chunks rather than into one buffer.

> It **MUST** bound the member count, and **MUST NOT** follow a symlink stored in an archive.

## 4. SQLite — Part 2 (GeoPackage)

**Dated correction, 2026-09-20 (adapter expansion phase 2).** The sentence below was true until
`adapters/geopackage.py` shipped; one adapter now opens a database, and how it meets each rule
follows the rules. The rules themselves are unchanged.

No shipped adapter opens a database: the same sweep for `sqlite3` returns nothing. *(superseded —
see the correction above)*

> A SQLite-reading adapter **MUST** open the file read-only through a URI —
> `file:…?mode=ro&immutable=1` — and never by plain path.

> It **MUST** call `enable_load_extension(False)`; an extension is arbitrary native code named by
> the file being read.

> It **MUST** set a query timeout and a progress handler, and **MUST** bound the row count it will
> read.

> It **MUST NOT** interpolate any value out of the file into SQL.

How `geopackage` meets them (`adapters/geopackage.py`, `_Snapshot`):

- **Read-only, and stronger than `mode=ro`**: the adapter never has a path. The caller supplies
  the octets, `sqlite3.Connection.deserialize` copies them into an in-memory connection
  (`sqlite3.connect(":memory:")` is the only `connect` in the module, held by a test), `PRAGMA
  query_only = ON` and `PRAGMA trusted_schema = OFF` are set before any schema-dependent
  statement, and an authorizer installed before the first read permits SELECT, READ on
  `sqlite_master`, the four `gpkg_*` core tables and the SELECTED feature tables, four functions
  (`count`, `length`, `max`, `typeof`) and one pragma (`table_xinfo`), and denies everything
  else — ATTACH, every write, every view, every other table, every other function. A test hashes
  the caller's bytes before and after. WAL-mode octets are refused as WAL-dependent unless the
  caller asserts a checkpointed snapshot, in which case a COPY has its two header octets
  rewritten and every object records the assertion.
- **`enable_load_extension(False)`** is called explicitly on the connection (the module's
  default is already off; a build without loadable-extension support has no method and nothing
  to disable), and `load_extension` is also denied by the authorizer's function allow-list.
- **Progress handler and bounds**: a progress handler aborts any statement past
  `GEOPACKAGE_MAX_SQL_STEPS` (20 000 000) virtual-machine steps — a step count rather than a
  clock, so the bound is deterministic; `PRAGMA quick_check(1)` runs once over the bounded
  input; the row count is bounded by `count(*)` before any row is fetched (`max_objects`
  10 000), every column's largest value by `max(length(…))` before any row is fetched (1 MiB),
  the coordinates across the package by a budget every WKB array debits before it is decoded
  (500 000), and the estimated output from the package block and the row count before any row
  is fetched (64 MiB). `max_input_bytes` (64 MiB) is the base class's, before `deserialize`.
- **No value from the file reaches SQL.** The only identifiers in any statement are table and
  column names read from `sqlite_master` and `PRAGMA table_xinfo`, validated (non-empty, no NUL,
  bounded length, no reserved prefix for a feature table) and double-quoted with `"` doubled;
  no caller-supplied SQL exists and no attribute value is ever interpolated. A generated or
  hidden column is refused rather than evaluated. Triggers never run because nothing is
  written; views are never selected from because the authorizer denies them.

## 5. Timeouts

> A decode **MUST** be bounded in wall time once `max_parse_seconds` is enforced, and an adapter
> **MUST** declare the bound or the reason it has none.

None of the nineteen enforces one today, and each says so in its own `absent_because`. The
conformance suite's checks **H** and **N** hold the related property — that a malformed or
truncated payload is refused *quickly* rather than hanging — with a per-case deadline that is
**enforced**: since audit finding F03 (2026-09-19) each case runs in a spawned worker process
that is terminated and killed when the deadline passes, and the case is `PARSER_TIMEOUT`, so a
parser that goes quadratic or never returns on a bad input is caught, and the sweep finishes,
even where no wall-clock bound is declared. A memory or CPU envelope for that worker is
Linux-only and is refused where it cannot be enforced — `docs/docs/security/deployment-envelope.mdx`
§2 has the platform table.

## 6. What the conformance suite verifies

| check | what it asserts |
|---|---|
| **H** — malformed payloads | every malformed fixture is refused, without a crash class, inside an enforced deadline in a killable worker; a `.json` payload past the loader's depth bound is refused by the loader, and the layer is recorded |
| **M** — streaming | `SKIP`, declared inapplicable, for all nineteen — never `PASS`: no adapter can declare a streaming capability, and the four properties one would consist of are printed as not implemented / not applicable |
| **N** — parser robustness | a truncated payload at many offsets is refused, inside the same enforced deadline |
| **O** — resource limits | one octet more than the declared `max_input_bytes` is refused; the payload *at* the bound is not refused by the size guard (`tests/test_cdm_resource_envelope.py`) |

**O is required in CI for all nineteen.** It was a SKIP for all fourteen of the day's adapters until round P5, because
no adapter declared a bound to check; it is now in the required set for every adapter in the
sweep, which is what would notice a bound being quietly removed from a manifest.

`max_input_bytes`, and they simply reach no parser this audit's grep can find, which is a property
of hand-written binary codecs rather than a gap in the audit.
